Now and then a word you already heard (and have not marked known) comes back 3–7 words later, with its own French ×3 and English.

Pictures come from Wikipedia, matched automatically to the English meaning of about 250 concrete nouns (animals, food, objects, places). Everything else gets no picture. If one is wrong, tap “Wrong picture”.

Backup & restore

Playback on iPhone: what to expect

Works: continuous listening while this page is open in Safari with the screen on. The app keeps the screen awake when Safari allows it.

Not guaranteed: playback with the screen locked or while another app is in front. A single web page that uses the voices built into the phone cannot promise that. If it stops, your place is saved: reopen and tap Resume.

If speech stalls: the app retries the same pronunciation up to three times, then stops and tells you. It never marks an unspoken word as heard and never skips one silently.

About the vocabulary & voices

All 3,011 preliminary vocabulary groups are included. Grouping was automatic, based on subtitle and TCF practice vocabulary. Ambiguous forms, names, and transcription artifacts remain. Dictionary glosses can have several meanings; the displayed English is a study gloss. Uncertain entries are labeled for review.

Translations adapted from WikDict, by Karl Bartel, using Wiktionary via DBnary, under CC BY-SA. The embedded adapted vocabulary data is available under the same license. Common meanings and study labels were edited for this player. In November 2026 every English gloss was reviewed and about 570 were corrected.

Pictures, when shown, are loaded from Wikipedia (Wikimedia) and are subject to their own licences; the page you open sends that site the English word being looked up. The audio is generated live using your device’s speech voices. It is not a prerecorded MP3. Depending on the voice, your device may use a network service. A French or English voice must be available for its language to play.
